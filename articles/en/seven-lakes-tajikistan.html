Haft Kul: An Introduction to the Seven Lakes

Just across the border from Samarkand, a chain of seven lakes climbs a narrow mountain valley. Locally they are called Haft Kul, “seven lakes” in Tajik, and they are one of the most rewarding nature trips you can make from Uzbekistan.

What makes the Seven Lakes of Tajikistan special is the contrast: bare, rust-colored cliffs rising straight out of water that shifts between deep green, blue and turquoise. The road between them passes villages, orchards and a fast mountain river, so the journey is part of the experience.

For travelers based in Samarkand, it is a chance to swap Silk Road architecture for high-mountain scenery, and add a second country, in one day.

Where Are the Seven Lakes Located?

The lakes lie in the Fann Mountains of Tajikistan, part of the Pamir-Alay range, in the valley of the Shing River south of the town of Penjikent (also spelled Panjakent). They sit at roughly 1,600–2,400 meters, each lake a little higher than the last.

Penjikent is a short drive from the Jartepa–Sarazm border crossing, about an hour from Samarkand by car, so a Samarkand to Seven Lakes trip fits into one long day.

The Seven Lakes of Tajikistan

The lakes formed behind natural rock dams along the Shing River. From the lowest to the highest, they are:

  • Mijgon — the first lake; its name means “eyelashes”.
  • Soya — “shadow”; steep cliffs keep it in shade for much of the day.
  • Hushyor — also written Gushor, a small lake framed by rocky slopes.
  • Nofin — “navel”, set deep in the valley.
  • Khurdak — “small”, one of the smaller lakes of the chain.
  • Marguzor — one of the largest, which also gives the area its other name, the Marguzor Lakes.
  • Hazorchashma — “a thousand springs”, the highest lake, known for its clear turquoise water.

Their colors change with the season, the weather and the light.

How to Get to Seven Lakes from Samarkand

Here is how a typical day trip works.

From Samarkand to the border

Trips usually start early, around 7:00, with a pickup from your hotel. The drive to the Jartepa–Sarazm border crossing takes about an hour.

Crossing the border

Travelers cross on foot, through Uzbek exit control and then Tajik entry control, where passports and entry documents are checked. This usually takes 20–40 minutes, longer when the crossing is busy. Rules and opening hours can change, so confirm them shortly before your trip.

Meeting your driver in Tajikistan

Most trips change vehicles at the border, so your Tajikistan-side driver meets you right after crossing. A 4x4 vehicle is generally used from here: the road up the valley is narrow, partly unpaved and steep, so good clearance and a driver who knows it matter.

Best Time to Visit Seven Lakes

Each season feels different:

  • Spring (April–May) — green slopes and blossom. Snowmelt can make the water less clear, and rockfalls or high water occasionally affect the road.
  • Summer (June–August) — usually the most reliable road and the brightest colors; cooler than Samarkand, with strong sun.
  • Autumn (September–October) — clear skies, golden trees and calm weather; for many travelers, the best time to go.

In winter, snow and ice can close the upper valley, so we recommend visiting between late spring and mid-autumn.

What to Expect During the Trip

A Seven Lakes day trip is about the whole journey into the mountains. Expect:

  • Mountain landscapes — canyon walls, river gorges and snow-capped peaks.
  • Traditional villages — small settlements and terraced gardens along the road.
  • Photography stops — short stops at the lakes to walk to the shore.
  • Local nature — clear streams, willows, poplars and fresh mountain air.

Many travelers buy fresh fruit at the bazaar in Penjikent on the way and enjoy a simple picnic by one of the upper lakes.

Travel Tips and Border Information

Passport and visa

Bring your passport. Depending on your nationality, you may enter Tajikistan visa-free, with an e-Visa, or only with a visa issued in advance. Also make sure you can re-enter Uzbekistan on the same day. Requirements change, so check the current rules for your nationality before you travel.

Clothing and shoes

Wear shoes with a good grip for rocky paths and bring a warm layer; mountain mornings can be cool even in summer.

What to bring

  • Cash in small notes (the local currency is the Tajik somoni).
  • Drinking water and snacks.
  • Sunglasses, a hat and sunscreen.
  • Any personal medication.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far are the Seven Lakes from Samarkand?

About an hour's drive to the Jartepa–Sarazm border, then usually around two hours through Penjikent and up the valley, depending on the road. Plan for a full day, returning to Samarkand in the late afternoon.

Can I visit all seven lakes in one day?

In good conditions, yes. A day trip usually includes short stops at each lake, ending at Hazorchashma, the highest one. Road and weather conditions can sometimes limit access to the upper lakes.

Do I need a visa to visit Tajikistan?

It depends on your nationality: some travelers enter visa-free, others need an e-Visa or a visa issued in advance. Check the current rules before your trip and make sure you can re-enter Uzbekistan.

Is a 4x4 vehicle necessary?

It is strongly recommended. The road up the valley is narrow and partly unpaved, and a 4x4 with an experienced local driver makes the journey safer and more comfortable.

What should I bring for the trip?

Your passport, cash in small notes, drinking water, comfortable shoes, a warm layer, sun protection and any personal medication.
