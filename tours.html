Samarkand in One Day

A private guide and air-conditioned car for the day, at your own pace — no set groups, no rushing.

1

Tour Program

Amir Temur Mausoleum (Gur-Emir). Begin your Samarkand journey at the tomb of the great conqueror Amir Temur. Discover his life story and the architecture of the Timurid era at one of Central Asia's most famous mausoleums.

Konigil Artisan Village. End the tour in a picturesque village where local craftsmen preserve the ancient tradition of hand-making Samarkand silk paper. Watch the entire production process and meet other traditional crafts.

In just one day, this tour reveals Samarkand's main landmarks, its centuries of history, and the unique charm of one of the greatest cities of the Great Silk Road.

Samarkand in Two Days

Two unhurried private days — the great monuments first, then the city's living crafts and science.

1

Day 1 — Tour Program

Amir Temur Mausoleum (Gur-Emir). Begin at the tomb of the great conqueror Amir Temur. Discover his life story and the architecture of the Timurid era at one of Central Asia's most famous mausoleums.

Registan Square. The main symbol of Samarkand and the heart of the ancient city. An ensemble of three majestic madrasas astonishes with its beauty, rich decoration and centuries-old history.

Bibi-Khanym Mosque. One of the largest mosques of its time, built on the orders of Amir Temur — a grand architectural monument wrapped in beautiful legends.

Hazrat Khizr Mosque. One of Samarkand's oldest mosques, perched on a hill above the Siab Bazaar with sweeping views over the old city.

Silk Road Samarkand. Finish at Samarkand's modern resort complex — landscaped gardens, fountains and architecture inspired by the region's Silk Road heritage.

1 DayBukhara

Bukhara Old City Discovery

A living museum of a city, where the Ark Fortress, Poi Kalyan minaret and centuries-old trading domes sit inside walking distance of each other.

1

Tour Program

Bolo-Hauz Mosque. The only surviving monument on Registan Square, facing the Ark fortress. The complex includes a pool, a Friday mosque and a minaret — its oldest part is the pool itself, whose name means "children's pond."

Ark Fortress. An ancient symbol of state power — this enormous fortress has stood at the heart of Bukhara for centuries as the seat and stronghold of its rulers.

Poi Kalyan Complex. Bukhara's central ensemble at the crossing of the "four bazaars" trade routes — Kalyan Mosque and Mir-i Arab Madrasa face each other with the towering Kalyan Minaret between them, alongside the Amir-Alimkhan Madrasa.

Ulugbek & Abdulaziz-Khan Madrasas. Built centuries apart — 1417 and 1652 — the two madrasas face each other as a single architectural ensemble; the Abdulaziz-Khan madrasa is among Central Asia's most richly decorated.

Ancient Trading Domes. A symbol of Bukhara, these domed bazaars ("taki") once rose at busy street crossings, sheltering merchant stalls and artisan workshops — only four domes survive today.

Magoki-Attori Mosque. One of the city's most mysterious monuments — before the Arab conquest this site held a spice bazaar and a temple to the Moon; its name means "pit," as centuries of accumulated soil have literally buried it below street level.

Lyabi-Hauz Complex. Bukhara's most beautiful and lively spot, built around a pool ("hauz") that has served as the city's social heart for centuries.

Individual tour with a private guide. Duration approx. 4 hours. Transfer from the station or airport — for an extra fee.

Tour Program

1 DayKhiva

Khiva: Ancient Traditions

A guided walk through Itchan Kala's ten essential monuments — the gates, minarets, mosques and palace of Uzbekistan's best-preserved walled city.

1

Tour Program

Ata-Darvaza Gate. The main entrance to Itchan Kala, whose name means "eight gates." This monumental 19th-century gate complex has symbolized the city's grandeur ever since.

Muhammad Amin-Khan Madrasa & Kalta Minor. An unfinished "short minaret" begun in the 19th century — work stopped when the ruler who commissioned it died, leaving its cylindrical form decorated with carved stone and mosaic.

Kunya-Ark Citadel. A fortress built in the 17th century by the ruler of Khiva, and for centuries the center of the city's political and cultural life.

Muhammad Rahim-Khan Madrasa. Built in 1871 under Khan Muhammad Rahim-Khan II, one of the finest examples of late-19th-century architecture in Uzbekistan.

Juma Mosque & Minaret. Built in 1788 and among the oldest mosques in Central Asia, its mudbrick prayer hall and courtyard reflect traditional Uzbek architecture.

Said Alauddin Mausoleum. A 14th-century mausoleum honoring the scholar Said Alauddin, decorated with mosaics and ceramic tilework and still a pilgrimage site today.

Pahlavan Mahmud Mausoleum. Built in 1913 and expanded over the years, this complex of mosque, madrasa and mausoleum holds the tomb of Pahlavan Mahmud alongside other noted Uzbek wrestlers and poets.

Islam Khodja Minaret & Madrasa. The tallest structure in old Khiva at 56.6 metres — among the tallest minarets in Central Asia, part of a complex with its namesake madrasa.

Ak Mosque (White Mosque). Built in 1838–1842 as an extension of the Juma Mosque complex, its white-brick facade in Kokand style spans roughly 55 by 46 metres.

Tash-Hovli Palace. The grand residence of Khiva's khans, blending Moorish, Persian and Russian influences across halls decorated with frescoes, carved wood and ceramic tile.

Individual tour with a private guide. Duration approx. 4 hours. Airport or train station transfer available for an extra fee.

1 DayTashkent

Tashkent City & Culture

A full day through Tashkent's monuments, mosques and modern squares — from the ancient Chorsu Bazaar to the grand Alisher Navoi Theater.

1

Tour Program

Monument of Courage. Erected in memory of the 1966 earthquake, a 26-metre column topped with a torch symbolizing hope.

Hast Imam Complex. Tashkent's spiritual heart: a mosque, the Barak-Khan madrasa, a library and the mausoleum of Hazrat Imam.

Chorsu Bazaar. A 2,000-year-old market beneath a striking blue dome, selling everything from fresh produce to traditional crafts.

Memorial to the Victims of Repression. Opened in 2002 to honor those killed under political repression from the 1920s to the 1950s.

Independence Square. The modern heart of the city, with fountains, gardens and monuments including one to Islam Karimov.

Romanov Palace. A grand early-20th-century residence built for the Governor-General of Turkestan, blending Russian and Central Asian styles.

Alisher Navoi Theater. Tashkent's premier opera and ballet house, founded in 1947 and named after the great Uzbek poet.

Amir Temur Square. A leafy park centered on an equestrian statue of Amir Temur, a favorite spot for locals and visitors alike.

1 DayShakhrisabz

Shakhrisabz: Birthplace of Timur

A scenic drive over the mountains to the hometown of Amir Temur, where an entire empire's ambition is written into a single ruined palace gate.

1

Tour Program

Tahtakaracha Pass. A mountain pass over 2,200m high on the road from Samarkand, known for its forests and sweeping valley views — and for being part of Temur's own route to his hometown.

Ak-Saray Palace. The towering, tile-covered ruins of Temur's 14th-century residence and ceremonial palace — today a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Dorus-Saodat Complex. A 15th-century complex built for Temur's family, combining a palace, a mosque and a mausoleum in one ensemble.

Dorut-Tillavat Complex. A 14th-century mausoleum ensemble for members of Temur's family, which also includes a mosque and a madrasa.

Kok-Gumbaz Mosque. A 15th-century mosque topped with a soaring turquoise dome, its grand prayer hall still in use today.

Lunch in the old town before the return drive to Samarkand in the evening.

1 DayTajikistan

Seven Lakes of Tajikistan

A full-day cross-border excursion into the Fann Mountains, where seven turquoise lakes of different shades step down a single valley. A valid passport is all you need — we handle the border crossing.

1

Tour Program

07:00 — Start of the tour. We pick you up from your hotel in Samarkand and drive towards the border with Tajikistan.

~08:00–08:15 — Jartepa border crossing. We cross the border on foot; the procedure usually takes about 20–40 minutes. On the Tajik side, a driver with a comfortable vehicle meets you and the journey continues.

Stop in Panjakent. A short stop at the local bazaar to buy fresh fruit for a small picnic later at the last lake.

The Seven Lakes (Haftkul). We drive into the picturesque Fann Mountains, reaching each of the seven lakes by car — Mijgon, Soya, Hushyor, Nofin, Khurdak, Marguzor and Hazorchashma — with a short stop at every lake to walk around, enjoy the scenery and take photos.

Picnic at the seventh lake. At Hazorchashma, one of the most beautiful spots in the valley, we usually stop for a small picnic with the fruit bought earlier in Panjakent.

Return. We drive back to the border, cross it on foot again, where a car is waiting on the Uzbek side. Around 16:00–17:30 we return to Samarkand and transfer you back to your hotel. Tour timing is approximate and may vary.

Important. This tour crosses an international border, so please carry your passport with you.
